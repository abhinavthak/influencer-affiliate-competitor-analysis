0.Summary

At a glance

Question
How are others paying people to promote online degrees, and what should our influencer affiliate programme for JGU Online look like?
Answer
Everyone runs one of five models (1). None of them runs a creator-first, performance-paid programme for one university with matched creators and approved claims. That is the gap (6).
Payout range
From about ₹5,000 per enrolment (alumni referral) to up to 30% of the programme fee (channel partners). Most figures are self-reported or come from third parties (4).
Biggest risk
Regulation. UGC bans franchise arrangements for online degrees, and education is ASCI's most non-compliant ad category (5).
Recommendation
A three-track programme: commission for small creators, a fixed fee plus commission for top creators, and a JGU alumni track. All under one approved-claims sheet and one tracking stack (7).
How to read
Sections 1 to 3 describe the market. Section 4 covers money, 5 the rules, 6 the gaps, 7 the plan. Section 8 lists what we still need to confirm.

0.1Why this matters

JGU Online offers about 35 online programmes, led by a 12-month MBA (7 specialisations) and a 3-year BBA. Its site has Apply Now buttons, brochure downloads, WhatsApp and a phone line. It has no referral or affiliate programme, and its links carry no tracking tags. Creators who talk about careers, finance and higher studies already influence degree choices. Today nobody tracks that influence or pays for it.

5.Rules that shape every model

5.1UGC: no franchising

  • UGC regulations say recognised institutions shall not offer online or distance programmes under any franchise arrangement. The institution keeps full responsibility.
  • In December 2022, UGC and AICTE asked institutions to end franchise arrangements with edtech companies. They specifically mentioned edtechs advertising degrees in newspapers, on social media and on TV.
  • A December 2023 notice warned against unapproved edtech tie-ups with foreign universities.
  • A legal commentary on these notices says ads must not imply recognition or accreditation that can't be proven.

5.2ASCI and CCPA: honest ads, clear disclosure

  • ASCI says education stayed the most non-compliant ad category in 2025, with 71 education ads flagged in the first half of FY26.
  • 98% of influencer ads ASCI reviewed needed changes, and 76% of India's top influencers broke disclosure rules.
  • Common breaches: "No. 1" and "most respected" claims with no third-party data, and promises of results.
  • CCPA fined Byju's ₹10 lakh in 2023 for misleading ads and issued guidelines on coaching ads in 2024.

5.3What this means for us

RuleHow the programme handles it
No franchisingJGU owns admissions, fees and the student relationship. We and our creators market only. The word "franchise" never appears in any contract or page.
No unproven claimsOne approved-claims sheet, signed off by JGU. JGU's site says "Institution of Eminence" and "India's #1 Private University". Creators use only that exact wording, with the source cited.
AccreditationNobody says "UGC approved" until JGU confirms it in writing (8).
DisclosureEvery post carries #ad or a paid-partnership label. We check each post before paying for it.
No guaranteesNo job, salary or "last few seats" claims. The creator brief bans them.

6.Gaps we can own

Each gap is something no competitor in section 2 does today.

1

Performance pay built for creators

Referral rewards are too small and flat fees carry no risk for the creator. Nobody offers creators a fixed fee plus a meaningful commission per enrolment.

Beats: sponsored content, refer and earn
2

Creators matched to programmes

Everyone promotes "an online MBA". JGU has niche degrees (public policy, ACCA, AI & data science, TESOL) whose audiences already follow specific creators.

Beats: portals, affiliate networks
3

Compliance as the selling point

With education the top ASCI violator, a university will pick the partner that guarantees approved claims, disclosure and review before content goes live.

Beats: Online Vidya, unmanaged affiliates
4

Proof from post to enrolment

Sponsored deals can't show which video led to which enrolment. Tracked links, promo codes and CRM status updates can, both for the creator and for JGU.

Beats: sponsored content, portals
5

Alumni as creators

Referral programmes treat alumni as one-off referrers. JGU alumni with a LinkedIn audience are the most trusted voices, and the cheapest to pay.

Beats: Amity, LPU refer and earn

7.Recommendation

7.1Positioning

Every enrolment traced to the creator who earned it, and every claim approved by JGU. We sell JGU better leads and lower risk. We offer creators clear tracking, payouts that arrive on time, and content support.

7.3Starting terms to test

  • Ask JGU for 15–25% of first-year fees per paid enrolment. That is below channel-partner rates and above affiliate-network rates (4.1).
  • Pay creators 40–60% of that. Our share covers counselling, tooling and refunds.
  • Pay after the refund window closes, like upGrad's affiliate rules. Credit lasts 30–90 days after the click.
  • Run the numbers in the calculator (4.2) once JGU's fee and commission are confirmed.

8.Open questions

  1. JGU commission. What does JGU pay per enrolment, by programme, and what are its first-year fees?
  2. Sub-affiliates. Does the JGU agreement let us pay outside creators to promote under JGU's brand?
  3. Accreditation wording. Which approvals can creators name, word for word (UGC entitlement, NAAC grade, AACSB)?
  4. Double credit. Do upGrad's affiliate-network partners already promote JGU programmes? If they do, which channel gets credit when both send the same student?
  5. Lead status from the CRM. Can the CRM send each lead's status (application, payment, refund) back to the tracking platform?
  6. Competitor gaps. Collect the referral terms for Online Manipal, NMIMS CDOE and Jain Online (2.7).
